An installed 0.1.8 updates itself: it shows "Restart to update" when the download is ready.

## Fixed

- **A crash while watching, when an audio device came or went.** Bloom 0.1.8 could quit on its own in the middle of a title when something changed among the Mac's audio devices: AirPods connecting, an AirPlay speaker appearing on the network. The cause was in libmpv's CoreAudio output, which kept a listener for device changes alive after the output itself was gone (mpv issue 18274). The two upstream fixes are in this release.
- **Sound goes through CoreAudio again on macOS 27.** On macOS 27 libmpv could not open its CoreAudio output for any title and fell back to AVFoundation without a word, and each failed attempt left behind the listener above. The fix from mpv's macOS maintainer (pull request 18637) is in this release. The output opens for mono, stereo, 5.1 and 7.1 sound, and a recording of a test clip comes out with the left and right channels where they belong.
- mpv's own warnings and errors now appear in Bloom's log, so a failure like this one is visible the first time it happens.

## Smoother 4K

A 4K title with nothing over the picture showed every frame on time already. A 4K HDR film at 3840 by 2160 did not: about one frame in eight came a refresh late, and the controls or a menu over a 4K picture cost a few more. The cause was the chroma upscaling in libmpv's renderer, two lanczos passes over the full frame on each video frame. Bloom now asks for bilinear chroma upscaling: on a 4K Dolby Vision film the GPU work per frame fell from 15 ms to 9 ms and no frame came late in a test of a thousand. Chroma is a quarter of the picture; the difference is not seen on a Mac screen.

## Transcodes that look as good and cost the server less

When the server must transcode a title (a file above its bitrate limit for remote clients, or a slow link), Bloom now asks for HEVC first and for a picture of at most 1080p. A transcode is 8-bit SDR in any case, and on a Mac screen a 1080p HEVC picture at the same bitrate is as good as a 4K one, while the server's encoder does a quarter of the work: a 4K episode starts in 2 seconds instead of 3 to 4, and the server stays minutes ahead of the playback instead of under a minute. Bloom also declares the HDR ranges it can show, so a server that honours them keeps the HDR of an HEVC transcode. A title that plays directly is not touched.

## For tests

`dev/live-check` plays titles of a server in a test instance and prints how each played: the play method, the time to the first frame, the pacing of the frames, stalls and a seek. `BLOOM_SERVER_URL` and `BLOOM_MPV_OPTS` are two more switches for a test instance.
